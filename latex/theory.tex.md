What you are describing—selecting a set of high-quality bases that are as orthogonal to each other as possible to span the feature space like principal components—is exactly what a Determinantal Point Process (DPP) does.You actually already have the fast greedy MAP inference for this implemented in your poison_eval.py under the --sel_dpp flag.Instead of just looking at pairwise distances (which FPS does, pushing points to extreme boundaries), DPP looks at the volume spanned by the entire set of chosen vectors.Here is the theoretical breakdown of how DPP achieves that "بردارهای یکه متعامد" (orthogonal unit vectors) behavior, formatted for your notes.Theoretical Motivation: Volume MaximizationIn linear algebra, the determinant of a Gram matrix (a matrix of dot products/cosine similarities between vectors) represents the squared volume of the parallelepiped spanned by those vectors.If the vectors are highly correlated, the volume collapses toward zero.If the vectors are perfectly orthogonal (like your desired independent bases), the volume is maximized.DPP elegantly combines this volume maximization with your quality scores to find a set that is both diverse (orthogonal) and high-quality (low score).Algorithm Steps: DPP Greedy MAP InferenceThe goal is to maximize the determinant of the submatrix $L_S$, defined as $L_{ij} = q_i q_j K_{ij}$, where $q$ is the quality and $K$ is the feature similarity kernel.1. Convert Scores to Quality Multipliers
Instead of using raw scores, transform them into a strictly positive quality scale $q$, where a lower score yields an exponentially higher quality.$$q_i = \exp(-\alpha \cdot (S_i - \min(S)))$$($\alpha$ controls the trade-off. High $\alpha$ ignores orthogonality and just picks the best scores; low $\alpha$ forces PCA-like orthogonal selection).2. Initialize the Orthogonal Tracking
Seed the set $\mathcal{S}$ with the candidate that has the absolute highest initial quality $q_i^2$ (which corresponds to the minimum score $S_i$).
Initialize a vector tracking the remaining orthogonal volume for each candidate (in your code, this is di2).3. Gram-Schmidt Orthogonalization (The "PCA" Step)
For every remaining candidate, you do not just check its distance to the chosen set. You project its feature vector onto the orthogonal complement of the subspace already spanned by $\mathcal{S}$.In your code, this is handled via Cholesky updates:$$e_i = \frac{L_{j} - c_{<k}^T c_i}{\sqrt{d_j^2}}$$This computes how much of candidate $i$'s vector is linearly independent from the previously chosen bases.4. Update and Select
Subtract the variance explained by the newly selected basis vector from all remaining candidates:$$d_i^2 \leftarrow \max(0, d_i^2 - e_i^2)$$Select the next candidate that maximizes this remaining orthogonal volume:$$j^* = \arg\max_{i \notin \mathcal{S}} d_i^2$$Repeat until $\vert{}\mathcal{S}\vert{} = N_p$.Why this is better than Filter/FPS for your use caseIf point $A$ is selected, FPS will pick point $B$ just because it's far away. If it then needs a third point, FPS might pick point $C$ which is far from $A$ and $B$, but lies entirely in the 2D plane already spanned by $A$ and $B$.DPP acts like PCA: it realizes $A$ and $B$ already cover that 2D plane, so it forces the selection of point $C$ to have a component in a third, orthogonal dimension, ensuring your poison bases capture genuinely independent features of the target class.
