\label{sec:selector-sensitivity}

\paragraph{Effect of DPP quality--diversity trade-off.}
The DPP parameter $\alpha$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:quality_term} controls the
relative emphasis on individual candidate quality and set diversity. We
evaluate $\alpha\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2,4\}$ while fixing the pointwise score
weight to $\lambda=1$. Random selection and Greedy provide two reference
points. We perform this ablation on dog--bird with SAPA using ConvNetBN
and ResNet20BN at a tight and a larger perturbation budget.

\paragraph{Effect of target-relevance weighting.}
The parameter $\lambda$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:selection-score} controls how
strongly target relevance contributes relative to boundary proximity.
We sweep $\lambda\in\{0,0.5,1,2,4,10,100\}$, where $\lambda=0$ removes the
target-specific representation term entirely. For every value of
$\lambda$, we evaluate both Greedy selection and DPP selection, allowing
us to separate the effect of the pointwise score from the additional
benefit of diversity-aware subset construction. DPP uses the default
$\alpha=2$ throughout this experiment, and Random provides a
$\lambda$-independent baseline.

\paragraph{Effect of surrogate ensemble size.}
Our selector averages boundary and representation statistics across
independently trained surrogate models. We vary the ensemble size
$K\in\{1,3,5,10,20,50\}$ while keeping all selection and attack
hyperparameters fixed at their default values. The $K=1$ setting
corresponds to selection from a single surrogate and therefore measures
the importance of cross-model consistency directly. We report the final
DPP selector together with Random selection as a common reference.
