\subsection{Empirical Validation of Residual Suppression}
\label{sec:residual-suppression-ablation}

We empirically examine the one-way implication of
Proposition~\ref{prop:residual-gating} at a fixed model state.  The study uses
the dog--bird setting with dog as the adversarial class and bird as the target
class.  We use ConvNetBN, five independently trained surrogate models, five
fixed targets, and all 5,000 adversarial-class training examples as candidate
bases.  This gives 25 target--surrogate pools and 125,000
candidate--surrogate evaluations.  The selector study uses $m=100$ poisons,
equivalently a poison fraction of $2\times10^{-3}$ relative to the full
training set, and the perturbation constraint is $\epsilon_\infty=8/255$.
Each selected set is optimized separately with GM and SAPA and evaluated using
five victim retrainings for each of the five targets, giving 25 victim trials
per attack and selector.

\paragraph{Candidate-level analysis.}
For every candidate $x_i$, target $x_t$, and surrogate, we record the signed
margin $M_i$, residual norm $\|r_i\|_2$, classifier-head interaction
\begin{equation}
H_i=(r_i^\top r_t)(h_i^\top h_t),
\label{eq:ablation-head-interaction}
\end{equation}
and backbone interaction
\begin{equation}
A_i=\left\langle
J_i^\top W^\top r_i,
J_t^\top W^\top r_t
\right\rangle.
\label{eq:ablation-backbone-interaction}
\end{equation}
The backbone interaction is computed exactly as the directional derivative of
the candidate loss along the target backbone gradient using a batched
Jacobian--vector product.  No explicit Jacobian matrix is constructed.  Within
each target--surrogate pool, we sort the 5,000 candidates by $M_i$ and divide
them into ten equal groups of 500 candidates.  We pool groups with the same
decile index only after this within-pool partition and report the median and
90th percentile of each magnitude.  Thus, every row of
Table~\ref{tab:residual-deciles} contains 12,500 evaluations and no target or
surrogate determines the decile boundaries of another pool.

The median and 90th percentile of all three quantities decrease monotonically
across all ten deciles in every one of the 25 target--surrogate pools.  From
the first to the ninth decile, the 90th percentiles of $\|r_i\|_2$, $|H_i|$,
and $|A_i|$ decrease by factors of approximately
$1.18\times10^5$, $9.27\times10^4$, and $9.74\times10^4$, respectively.
As a numerical sanity check, all 125,000 candidate--surrogate evaluations
satisfy the residual upper bound in
Eq.~\eqref{eq-margin-residual-sandwich}.

\paragraph{Controlled selector intervention.}
We next examine whether candidates from the residual-suppressed regime remain
poor bases even when they are selected using the other score components.  All
quantities are standardized separately within the candidate pool of each
surrogate and then averaged across the five surrogates.  We compare
\begin{align}
s_i^{\mathrm{full}}
&=
\widetilde R_i-\widetilde M_i+\widetilde A_i,
&
s_i^{\mathrm{no\text{-}M}}
&=
\widetilde R_i+\widetilde A_i.
\label{eq:residual-selector-controls}
\end{align}
The Full and No-margin selectors take the $m$ largest corresponding scores
over the complete candidate pool.  The High-margin control first restricts the
pool to the highest quartile of the ensemble-averaged standardized margin and
then selects the $m$ largest values of $s_i^{\mathrm{no\text{-}M}}$ within that
quartile.  It therefore chooses the strongest available representation and
backbone scores subject to an intentionally unfavorable margin regime.  The
same targets, surrogate checkpoints, poison budget, attack settings, and
victim identifiers are used for all three selectors.

The High-margin control has representation relevance comparable to both
unrestricted selectors, but its residual norm is 149 times smaller than that
of Full selection.  Its classifier-head and backbone interactions are 107 and
123 times smaller, respectively.  The resulting poisons achieve zero ASR for
both GM and SAPA, whereas Full selection achieves $32\%$ and $28\%$.  Clean
accuracy remains similar across selectors, so this loss of attack success is
not explained by a collapse in standard victim accuracy.  The Full and
No-margin sets overlap by an average of 91.6 of their 100 bases, which explains
their similar interaction statistics and attack outcomes.  No margin is
slightly stronger in this small study, so these results do not establish that
the explicit margin term improves the ranking of candidates outside the
suppressed regime.  Instead, they support the narrower role established by the
proposition, namely that a sufficiently large positive margin identifies
candidates whose residual suppresses both components of the local gradient
alignment.

This experiment is a mechanism study rather than an independent proof of the
proposition or a validation of the complete bilevel objective.  The attack
results cover one architecture, one class pair, one poison budget, and five
targets.  We therefore use them as a controlled illustration of residual
suppression and do not infer that the margin alone guarantees attack success
for the remaining candidates.
